# Limitations

- **Four-state abstraction:** product views, prices, quantity, and customer context collapse into only browsing, cart, purchase, and exit.
- **Inferred exits:** the source does not provide an exit event. EXITED is added at the end of a session without a transaction.
- **30-minute session boundary:** this configurable inactivity threshold is a convention and may split or combine real visits incorrectly.
- **Identity and session quality:** visitor IDs can represent browsers or devices rather than people, and missing IDs are excluded.
- **Dataset bias and age:** the source is a historical, selected e-commerce dataset; its patterns need not match current stores or shoppers.
- **Missing attributes:** the model cannot condition on demographics, products, campaign, device, or inventory.
- **First-purchase terminal assumption:** later activity and repeat purchases within a session are ignored after the first transaction.
- **Simulation versus prediction:** simulation describes paths generated by fitted probabilities; it does not establish causal effects or guarantee real-world accuracy.
- **First-order Markov assumption:** the next state depends only on the current state, ignoring prior path, time spent, and other context.
- **Train/test differences:** a chronological split can expose temporal drift; rare states may not occur in training, making a row unsupported.
- **No smoothing:** zero empirical transitions remain zero. This keeps estimates transparent but makes sparse data brittle.
- **Memory:** required columns are loaded at once. Chunked reading with cross-chunk session continuity is not implemented.
- **Outcome comparison:** Monte Carlo variation and small test sets can make simulated proportions differ from observed proportions.
